## How to Verify Your Hotel’s Safety Before Booking

When you are traveling solo, your hotel is your sanctuary. Don't just rely on pretty photos; look for the details that actually keep you secure.

- **The "Main Street" Rule:** Always check the map. Is the hotel tucked away in a dark, narrow alleyway, or is it positioned on a busy, well-lit street? A hotel with high foot traffic at night is significantly safer.
- **24/7 Reception:** For solo travelers, having a physical person at the front desk 24 hours a day is non-negotiable. It provides peace of mind knowing there is always someone on-site to assist you.
- **Internal Hallways:** Opt for hotels where room doors open into an internal hallway rather than directly onto the street. This adds an extra layer of privacy and security.

**Q: How can I verify if a hotel is truly located in a safe neighborhood?**
A: Aside from reading recent reviews from other solo female travelers, use the TripMaker "Safety & Main Street" filter. It automatically surfaces hotels with the best proximity to transit and well-lit, high-traffic areas, saving you the time of cross-referencing maps manually.

**Q: What is the most important thing to do when I arrive at my hotel?**
A: Always test your room’s door lock and deadbolt immediately upon entry. If the room has a balcony or a ground-floor window, ensure those are also secure. Keeping your room number private during check-in and having your hotel’s address saved in your offline maps are also essential safety habits.
